Loop

How to make a GIF loop forever, or play just once

Updated October 21, 2026, 4 min read

Quick answer

Loop count is stored inside the GIF file. In Make Video to GIF open GIF settings and set the loop to infinite for reactions, or to a fixed number of plays for demos, then export. A GIF that stops after one play was exported without the loop setting; re-export it from the video with an infinite loop.

Whether a GIF loops is not decided by the app showing it but by a small setting inside the file, the loop count. Zero means forever; no setting at all usually means play once.

There are two separate goals here: making the file loop, and making the loop look seamless. Both are easy once you know where they come from.

Loop forever, once, or a few times

Choose by where the GIF will be seen:

Loop settingUse for
InfiniteReactions, memes, README demos, anything people glance at
Play onceA reveal or an ending that should stay on the last frame
2–3 timesStep-by-step demos in docs or emails, so motion stops after a moment

Viewers have historically disagreed on whether a loop count of 1 means one play or one extra play; test where the GIF will be shown.

Make the loop seamless

A loop looks seamless when the last frame flows into the first. Trim to a stretch that starts and ends in a similar position: a head nod, a wave, a spinning object, a repeating UI animation. Avoid cutting in the middle of a camera move.

Some platforms (Telegram, X and others) convert GIFs to short videos and loop them regardless of the loop count, so a play-once GIF may loop there anyway.

Tips

  • Infinite loops are the safe default for chat apps.
  • For a demo GIF in an email, two or three plays avoid distracting the reader.
  • Projects can be reopened in Make Video to GIF, so trying another loop setting doesn't mean starting over.

Questions people also ask

Why does my GIF only play once?

It was exported without a loop count, which most viewers treat as play once. Export it again with an infinite loop.

Can I stop a GIF from looping?

Yes. Set the loop to play once before exporting. Some apps that convert GIFs to video may still loop it.

How do I make a perfect loop?

Trim so the first and last frames match, keep the camera still, and use an infinite loop.
